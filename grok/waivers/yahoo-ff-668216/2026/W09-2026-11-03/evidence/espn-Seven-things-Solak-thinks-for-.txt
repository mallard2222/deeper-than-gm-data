It's the last week before the trade deadline next Tuesday at 4 p.m. ET. It's also the official midway point of the season. Feel that little bite in the air? Playoff football will soon cometh. Every Tuesday of the NFL season , I'll turn the page from the previous week to the upcoming one with seven things I think. These aren't full-scale predictions. They aren't concrete takeaways from the past week of NFL action, either; those are fired off Sunday or Monday. Instead, these are the uncertain topics. They are things that might be true, but it'll probably take the next few games to find out. And at the bottom, you'll find a few extra half-thoughts ... just those little inklings I'm unable to shake. He is a special player who can have the same impact on a healthy offense that Saquon Barkley had for the Eagles in 2024 and Derrick Henry for the Ravens in the same year. Barkley and Henry had rushing success rates of 35.4% and 37.8%, respectively, and averaged 4.2 and 4.3 yards per carry, respectively, in the two seasons before they joined their new squads. Over the past two seasons, Taylor has a 41.2% success rate and is averaging 4.7 yards per pop. His recent body of work, along with the glimmer of elite play we saw in the early parts of the 2025 season, indicates that he can be a transformative addition. NFL Network's Ian Rapoport and ESPN's Adam Schefter reported that the Colts' asking price is something in line with the Christian McCaffrey trade package from 2023 -- a second-round pick, a third-round pick and some more capital. If Taylor gets dealt for that much compensation to a team such as the Vikings, Giants, Broncos or Bengals (or a surprise team such as the Packers and Seahawks), it's possible the running back trade market will freeze up. If the Colts get that much in return for Taylor, what could the Titans get for Tony Pollard or the Jets for Breece Hall ? Jets head coach Aaron Glenn shut down trade speculation involving Hall after the 2-6 Jets' loss to the Raiders this past week, saying, "We're in the business of keeping good players, and he's one of our better players. So, Breece will still be here." I'm inclined to believe Glenn. Then again, Glenn could be on his way out, and I don't think general manager Darren Mougey would lament making Glenn look silly by trading Hall should he receive a big offer from a team that misses out on Taylor. Similarly, I find the reports that the Eagles would never trade Barkley reasonable, but not absolute. Barkley was asked if the front office told him he'd be an Eagle beyond the deadline , and he responded, "It was mentioned to me." Of course, that's before the Eagles lost another game -- to the 1-4-1 Commanders. I would put the chances of Barkley not getting traded somewhere between 95-99%, but Roseman is the sort of GM to never say never. Denver is entrusting a lot of backfield snaps to a fourth-round rookie in Jonah Coleman ( whom I like quite a bit) , but the Broncos need their running game to be more dangerous given the limitations on the Bo Nix -led passing attack. Quarterback Kyler Murray was Minnesota's leading rusher against the Lions, as the Vikings struggle to get their running game going to open their play-action pass attack. Josh Jacobs returned for the Packers from the commissioner exempt list, but he still might be suspended and hasn't been the same since coming back. The Bengals are looking for an upgrade over Chase Brown , as are the Giants with Cam Skattebo , whom they don't seem to trust to be a three-down player. But the market is paralyzed by Taylor, as all these teams stare down the Colts and their towering demands. George Pickens forces his way out of Dallas by the deadline I understand academically that the 3-5 Cowboys are not planning to trade anyone this deadline. Executive vice president Stephen Jones said on the radio this week that they're open to acquiring a player at the deadline to help salvage this season. I think that is about as short-sighted an approach as can be. The Cowboys are already massively leveraged against their future draft capital. Thanks to the Micah Parsons trade, Dallas still has a first-round pick in the 2027 NFL draft . However, that pick will be the worst of either the Cowboys' or Packers' selection -- and Green Bay looks like a team that might pick in the high 20s this year. The Cowboys are missing a third, a fifth and a sixth in the upcoming draft after the trades for Broderick Jones , Pickens and Joey Porter Jr . -- and are down a 2028 second-round pick as well. Dallas has 10 total picks over the next two drafts; the next closest are the Rams, Texans and Bears with 12 apiece. The Cowboys are not projected to earn compensatory selections in the 2027 draft, so no extra picks are coming. The Cowboys can't cut much payroll in the upcoming seasons because they lack the draft capital. play 1:50 Stephen A. proposes a Steelers reunion with George Pickens Over the Cap has the Cowboys 22nd out of 32 teams in 2027 cap space and 25th in 2028 cap space. Dallas' books are laden with veteran contracts. By average annual value, the Cowboys have the second-most expensive quarterback ( Dak Prescott ), the sixth-most expensive receiver ( CeeDee Lamb ), the 10th-most expensive tight end ( Jake Ferguson ), the second-most expensive guard ( Tyler Smith ), the second-most expensive defensive tackle ( Quinnen Williams ) and the 10th-most expensive cornerback ( DaRon Bland ). Oh, and the most expensive kicker ( Brandon Aubrey ). These contracts give the Cowboys an opportunity to convert base salary into bonus money and create short-term cap space -- a technique they've largely avoided, but deployed earlier this season when they restructured the deals for Ferguson, Bland and defensive tackle Kenny Clark . Those players are also three of the biggest veteran disappointments on an underwhelming roster. The Cowboys' future spending is so significant because Dallas has two enormous rising free agents: Pickens and Porter. Pickens was acquired after the 2025 draft. The Cowboys did not sign him to an extension before his career-best 93-catch, 1,429-yard season and franchise-tagged him shortly thereafter. Pickens is playing on the tag (worth $27.3 million) and will either hit free agency or receive the second franchise tag (worth about $32.8 million) in 2027. The Cowboys can't swallow another $33 million on their 2027 cap -- certainly not if they'd like to retain the tag to use on Porter, who is also playing without an extension and would like to be paid upwards of $30 million per year. Remember: they get only one tag per year. Can the Cowboys extend Pickens at a market rate? That'll be, conservatively, over $33 million APY -- worryingly close to Lamb's $34 million per year . Not only might that number introduce some uneasiness in the Cowboys' wide receiver depth chart, but they would be paying it to a player who has cleared 100 yards just once this season and is known for declining effort and interest when he isn't getting the football. I can't figure out how it makes sense for the Cowboys to keep Pickens on their roster in 2027. The money doesn't add up from a cap-management perspective, nor does the on-field opportunity and impact at that contract rate. And if the Cowboys' ownership doesn't see this, perhaps Pickens will. Bucky Irving will lead the Buccaneers to an NFC South title Bucky is back! There are few players as fun to watch as Bucky Irving. Pure juice. He's quicker and stronger than you realize. When Irving hit as a rookie in 2024 -- a fourth-round scatback suddenly capable of enduring a full workload of 15 to 20 touches -- he was the surprise engine that powered then-offensive coordinator Liam Coen's head coaching bid and Baker Mayfield 's career renaissance. The Buccaneers ranked in the top five in pretty much every offensive metric -- points per drive, EPA per play, success rate and EPA per drive. Then disaster struck. Irving injured his foot and shoulder in Week 4 of the 2025 season, missed seven games and was a shadow of himself when he returned. Irving's shoulder required offseason surgery, and his availability for camp and 2026 Week 1 was, at times, in doubt. These past few games have been a reminder of what Irving looks like at full strength. Over the past four weeks, Irving leads the league in carries (91) and rushing yards (487). He is one of nine players (minimum 50 carries) averaging at least 5 yards per rush, and his teammate Kenny Gainwell is one of the other eight. Irving and Gainwell are such a fun one-two punch because they both have quickness behind the line of scrimmage, but neither fears contact or makes tepid decisions. This allows the Buccaneers to run a lot of gap-scheme concepts; NFL Next Gen Stats has them running more power and counter runs than any other offense, save for the Texans. Irving and Gainwell have the decisiveness to hit the hole quickly when it opens, but also the agility to bounce to a backside when the intended gap gets gummed up. It's Gainwell. Did I get you? Gainwell and Irving's redundancy might at first seem counterintuitive, but it actually helps the Bucs from a scheme perspective. Because both can catch passes, the Buccaneers can line them up on interchangeable downs without tipping run or pass pre-snap. Of course, with smaller backs, the concern is overuse. Irving has 26 games since the start of 2024 with at least 15 touches -- among sub-200-pound backs, only James Cook III and De'Von Achane have more. But Gainwell is fourth with five such games, and the fact that both can be trusted for 15-plus touches allows them to spell one another well. The entire playbook is open for the Buccaneers with either of their backs on the field. It's hard to run the ball on 92% of your offensive plays, as the Bucs did in their historic 16-0 win over the Falcons in the rain Sunday, without that deep of a bag of plays and a highly effective RB2. As they roll forward, the Buccaneers will continue to use Gainwell liberally, but the heartbeat of this team is once again Irving, as it was in 2024. This offensive line, which was shaky in September, is rounding into form as the Buccaneers get a career-best season from center Graham Barton and a return to health for right guard Cody Mauch . The duo of left tackle Tristan Wirfs and right tackle Luke Goedeke remains among the league's elite despite minimal fanfare. Wirfs especially has a Trent Williams -esque effect on the Buccaneers' rushing game: They average 5.8 yards per carry on runs to the left. Only the 49ers are higher, at 5.9. The Bucs have two blocking tight ends in Payne Durham and Bauer Sharp -- the latter is a sixth-round rookie who is emerging as a stud point-of-attack player -- who allow them to get even more diverse in their backfield alignments. All of the pieces are in place for the Buccaneers to win on the ground week after week -- perhaps not for 324 yards every time, of course. I think ... the Steelers are going to win the AFC North I just told you how I think the 3-5 Buccaneers will win the NFC South because they can run the ball and play great defense. I think the 6-2 Steelers will win the AFC North because they can run the ball and play great defense. This Steelers' rushing attack is the stuff of fever dreams. They lead the league in EPA per rush (0.11) and are second in explosive rush rate (10.9%). Jaylen Warren is enjoying a career-best season. He's second in the league in rushing yardage and rushing yardage over expectation, and leads the league in yards per carry at 5.9. It's easy to think of the Steelers as a heavy-personnel team, but it isn't the case. The Steelers are in multi-tight end sets only 33% of the time (league average is 37.1%), and they rarely use two-back sets. The Steelers are at their best out of 11 personnel (three receiver sets). They're averaging 5.3 yards per carry, which ranks seventh in the NFL. Of course, when the one tight end on the field is the 6-foot-7 and 300-something-pound Darnell Washington , 11 personnel can feel like a jumbo set with an additional offensive lineman. But Washington doesn't tip the scales so dramatically. In fact, the Steelers average 5.9 yards per rush when Washington is off the field (compared with 4.6 when he's on it). This is all about the Steelers' line, which is one of the only groups in the league with an average starter or better at all five spots. The Steelers switched up the right side of their offensive line before Week 3, subbing rookie tackle Max Iheanachor in for Dylan Cook and starting Brock Hoffman for injured right guard Spencer Anderson . Since Week 3, they have led the league in yards per carry and runs for positive yards over expectation. To that right side, they're averaging a whopping 6.2 yards per carry on 11.2 carries per game. The Steelers devastated the Browns in Week 8 with trap runs all game, weaponizing the Browns' attack-style front against them but inviting defensive tackles upfield -- then hit them with thunderous strikes from incoming linemen. Watch as the Steelers' interior intentionally leaves defensive tackles unblocked to climb up to the second level, creating big avenues for Warren and Rico Dowdle to pick their way into open space. pic.twitter.com/X8Rqm5vz7B — Good Clips (@MeshSitWheel) November 3, 2026 The Steelers' passing-attack numbers are still worryingly poor -- they rank bottom five in success rate and explosive play rate -- but veteran quarterback Aaron Rodgers deserves field general credit. He gets Pittsburgh out of bad run looks and into good ones by constantly audibling at the line of scrimmage. Because the Steelers can dominate on the ground, they can salt away moderate leads even with a lack of big-play scoring. They have the running game for it, and they have the defense. Seven different Steelers players had at least two pressures Sunday as Pittsburgh ripped through the Browns' offensive line. The Steelers sacked quarterback Deshaun Watson eight times. The Jets also sacked Watson eight times in Week 5 -- he is the only quarterback in the league to have been sacked more than six times in a game this season. New defensive coordinator Patrick Graham has introduced a level of unpredictability that was not present in the Steelers' defense for many seasons. The Steelers are rushing a defensive back -- namely, Jalen Ramsey -- on 12% of opponent dropbacks. That's their highest number since 2020, when Mike Hilton was still on the team. They've lined up No. 3 edge rusher Nick Herbig in a wider variety of alignments. He was once primarily a right edge rusher, but now he flip-flops sides and spends several snaps as a walked-up spinner in the A-gaps. With a greater variety of defensive alignments comes more diverse coverages as well. The Steelers are running single-high coverage on 44% of snaps this year after averaging 63.4% in the five seasons previous -- the most of any defense over that span. They ran 28% man free in that five-year period as well -- fourth among all defenses. This season, they're at 9% -- the second lowest, ahead of only the Vikings. This is the modernization for which many Steelers fans screamed over the past few years. Graham isn't reinventing any schematic wheels. He's just running far more modern stuff than Mike Tomlin and Teryl Austin's units were, and that refurbishing has eliminated some of the easy completions the Steelers surrendered in previous years. A strong day against the Browns' offense does not make an elite defense. But this season, the Steelers are fifth in defensive EPA per drive and fifth in points per drive allowed. Not only am I confident that this is a top-10 defense, but I really like the Steelers' model of a dominant running game and stiff defense in an AFC playoff field littered with elite quarterbacks and suspect defenses. Pittsburgh will be a tricky postseason opponent as long as the passing game gets semi-functional by season's end because the Steelers can drag typically high-scoring offense into a muddy affair. To do so, they might have to start with the division-rival Bengals, whom they've already beaten once this season. The Steelers have a half-game lead in the division over the 5-2 Bengals and the Week 3 head-to-head win that featured a decisive muffed Cincinnati punt in the fourth quarter. ESPN's FPI gives the Steelers a 24% chance to win the division relative to the Bengals' 73%, thanks in large part to the remaining strength of schedules. The Bengals have the 32nd-toughest schedule remaining, whereas the Steelers are 13th. But don't worry about Week 15 matchups because a lot can change between now and then. The Bengals host the Steelers on Sunday night in Week 10. Pittsburgh will be coming off a bye week, and the Bengals will be travelling back from their Madrid game against the Falcons in Week 9. A post-travel game against a rested opponent is not an easy task. If the Steelers win that game, they'll own the head-to-head tiebreaker and at least a 1½-game lead. The Bengals have a terrifying passing attack, but without a balanced running game, they can't salt away leads. Their defense, especially on the ground, has shown cracks during the past several weeks after a red-hot start. The Steelers are well-equipped to beat them. The Browns and Ravens don't have the quarterback play to keep pace. I think this is Pittsburgh's division. Herbert reminds me of my childhood desktop computer, which would become so unusable if more than three programs were open that nothing but a hard reboot would save it. There was no little tweak -- control-alt-delete, close a window or two or clear some memory. It was best to press the power button and restart. This is most evident in Herbert's interceptions. Herbert is throwing blithely into unmoving defenders with the blind certainty of a QB3 undrafted free agent forced into NFL action. I have never seen, or can I remember, a quarterback start for six seasons ... and then produce plays this perplexingly poor. This moment is the absolute valley of Herbert's value in the league. Of course, Goff became so much more in Detroit. Stafford was 33 when the Rams acquired him -- Herbert is five years younger. Stafford had two years left on his deal; Herbert has four. Campanile, similar to most defensive coaches, might not be an obvious head coaching candidacy -- the league tends to hunt for offensive stars, such as young Jaguars offensive coordinator Grant Udinski. But Campanile has the right lineage. He coached linebackers in Miami under Brian Flores for two seasons, then was retained in Miami by Mike McDaniel and coached under Vic Fangio in 2023. Campanile left Miami to join Jeff Hafley's staff in Green Bay before snagging the Jaguars' defensive coordinator job -- his first playcalling spot at the NFL level -- in 2025. Every linebacker whom Campanile has coached turned to gold. Devin Lloyd 's 2025 explosion in Jacksonville was one of the least probable breakouts I've seen over the past decade of football. Quay Walker and rookie Edgerrin Cooper meshed with Campanile's coaching in Green Bay in 2024. Jerome Baker broke out in Miami in his first season under Flores and Campanile. Good positional coaching is a sign that Campanile is a strong communicator; he knows how to engage players. Now consider how well he has managed the defense as the playcaller in Jacksonville. Since the start of last season, the Jaguars are third in EPA per drive and fourth in points per drive allowed. They're doing this with solid players, but not stars. They don't have a defensive player signed to a top-10 contract for their position. Josh Hines-Allen , the highest-paid Jaguars defender, is 11th by average annual value among edge rushers. The fourth-highest-paid Jaguars defender is not even exclusively a defender: It's Travis Hunter , who is paid $11.66 million after being the No. 2 pick. The best defensive coordinators are not system defensive coordinators; you can't copy and paste the Flores system and deploy it with your personnel the way you might with a Shanahan-style offense. Defensive playcalling requires more week-over-week morphing, as offensive problems constantly change and require new solutions. The best defensive coordinators have thick toolbelts who can find that one thingamajig that will solve the leaky faucet or jammed door. Campanile has got a special blend in Jacksonville. The Jaguars will pressure much like Flores, sending blitzers from every side of the formation while dropping into split-field zone coverages that allow them to drive on routes quickly. They also play a lot of quarter-quarter-half and cloud coverages in the Fangio style to get numbers over both sides of the formation and free up weakside safeties to make plays on the ball. To solve the issue of cloud coverages leading to light boxes, they maximize the value of tweener players such as defensive tackle Arik Armstead and edge rusher Travon Walker by constantly stunting their front.
